# Short Title

## Problem

The motivation, written to stand without the solution: what is wrong or
missing, what it costs, and what any fix must guarantee.

## Design

The intended change. Future or prescriptive tense while proposed or
accepted; present-tense shipped reality once active. Free-form technical
subsections are fine (interfaces, formats, wiring) — every behavior stated
must be checkable from the acceptance criteria below.

## Alternatives considered

Mandatory. Each genuine alternative and why it lost — one bold-led paragraph
per alternative, or a `### Why not <X>?` subsection per contested one.
Alternatives are recorded, never invented. A decision recorded without what
it beat invites re-litigation.

## Acceptance criteria

Bullets of specific, testable criteria that determine whether the spec is
correctly and completely implemented.

## Testing

What the unit tests must cover (modules, cases, fixtures). Omit only for
non-code projects, where the acceptance criteria are the verification.

## Open questions

While `status: proposed`. Every question must be answered — with the answer
documented in place — before the flip to `accepted`; in an `accepted` spec
every item is already answered in place. The section may stay once everything
is answered, for reference.
